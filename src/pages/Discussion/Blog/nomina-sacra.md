<p>Nommy what? Oh, it’s Latin and it means ‘Sacred Names’ and they’re a not-very-well-known feature of our earliest Greek manuscripts. We need a photo here:</p>

<figure><a href="/assets/essays/nomina-sacra.svg"><img src="/assets/essays/nomina-sacra.svg" alt="Four boxed abbreviations with overlines in Mark 1: Jesus, Messiah, God, and Master, in Codex Washingtonianus." loading="lazy" /></a></figure>

<p>Mark begins with: The_beginning of_the good_message of_<strong class="sacred-name">Yaʸsous</strong> <strong class="sacred-name">messiah</strong>, son of_<strong class="sacred-name">god</strong>. <sup><span style="color: #c9211e">2</span></sup> As it_has_been_written in the Aʸsaias the prophet: Behold, I_am_sending_out the messenger of_me before the_face of_you, who will_be_preparing the way of_you. <sup><span style="color: #c9211e">3</span></sup> The_voice of_a_shouting one in the wilderness: Prepare the way of_the_<strong class="sacred-name">master</strong>, be_making the paths of_him straight. (Mark 1:1-3, <em>OET</em> Literal Version)</p>

<p>The bolded words are marked as <a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nomina_sacra"><em><u>nomina sacra</u></em></a> in the manuscript and the two sets of boxes match in the same order. If you look carefully at the highlighted letters in the Greek manuscript, you’ll notice that they all have lines above the Greek letters. They are actually abbreviations of the Greek words Ἰησοῦ (Iēsou=Jesus) Χριστοῦ (Ⱪristou=Christ/Messiah), Θεοῦ (Theou=God), and Κυρίου (Kuriou=boss/master/lord). Each of those Greek words which are 5-7 letters long, is abbreviated to just two Greek letters with an overline. In the earliest manuscripts, this appears to indicate that the scribes believed the words to refer to God. (We know this, because when the same the Greek word obviously refers to ‘a god’ or ‘a false god’, it’s not marked as <em>nomina sacra</em>.)</p>

<p>Most English translations, even very literal ones, don’t mark the words that the scribes marked as <em>nomina sacra</em>, however, the <em>Open English Translation</em> marks them in the text in <strong>bold</strong>, as we aim to advance the art of Bible translation.</p>
